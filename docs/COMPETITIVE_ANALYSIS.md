# TradeVerified — Competitive Analysis & Winning Strategy

A breakdown of who else is in the "trades hiring + verification" space, **who they charge**, and exactly where TradeVerified should position itself to win. Ends with the "ultimate platform" feature set that combines the best of every competitor.

---

## 1. Who charges whom

The single most important strategic decision in a two-sided marketplace is *which side pays*. Here's how the field breaks down.

| Platform | Workers pay? | Employers pay? | Model notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **BlueRecruit** | ❌ Free | ✅ Yes | Free for job seekers + small businesses; larger employers ~$300/mo. Strong trade-school pipeline. |
| **TradesCraftConnect** | Partial (Pro tier) | ✅ Yes | Free base for workers, paid "Pro"; employers pay to post/hire. Crew-building + urgent hire. |
| **Blue Collar Social** | ❌ Free | ✅ Yes | Workers + homeowners free; companies pay to recruit. Network/feed angle. |
| **Hard Hat Social** | ❌ Free (premium planned) | (early) | Manual credential review; free directory now, premium later. |
| **go2work** | ❌ Free | ✅ Yes | AI matching; employer-paid hiring. |
| **ProGuild.ai** | ✅ Flat monthly | ❌ (homeowners free) | Pros pay a flat fee, **zero lead fees**; homeowner marketplace. |
| **PROHire** | ❌ Free | ✅ Yes | Automated license verification; employer-paid. |
| **SiteReady** | ❌ Free | ✅ 12% booking fee | Transaction fee on bookings; federal/public-works niche. |
| **TradesLink** | ❌ Free | ✅ / Sponsored | Community + employer connections; association-backed. |
| **Indeed / LinkedIn** | ❌ Free | ✅ Yes | Generic; not trade-aware (no license logic). |

### The clear pattern
**Almost nobody charges workers to find a job.** The winners charge **employers** (subscription or transaction fee). The one model that charges pros (ProGuild) is a *homeowner lead-gen* marketplace, not a job board — and even it brags about "zero lead fees" to soften it.

**Takeaway for TradeVerified:** Your instinct was correct. Keep it **free for tradespeople to find work**, monetize **employers**, and treat the worker "Pro" tier as a pure optional upgrade (visibility/branding), never a paywall on jobs.

## 2. What each competitor does well (steal these)

- **BlueRecruit** — skills/cert-based matching (no resume), trade-school graduate pipeline, in-app chat + video interviews, fast first-day connections.
- **TradesCraftConnect** — crew builder (multi-hire), 24-hour "urgent hire" blast, map view of nearby available workers, interview scheduler, apprentice path.
- **Blue Collar Social** — true professional-network feel: feed, community, reputation that "does the selling for you," homeowners + companies + workers in one place.
- **Hard Hat Social** — *manual* credential review (no self-certification), trade-specific requirements (AWS for welders, EPA 608 for HVAC, state license for electricians/plumbers).
- **go2work** — AI matching + background checks, strong mobile app.
- **ProGuild.ai** — **state-license-board verification**, "ProGuild Score," ID + background check on signup, zero lead fees messaging.
- **PROHire** — automated verification by integrating with licensing boards, visual portfolios, proximity-based smart matching.
- **SiteReady** — deep compliance trail (55+ credentials), verify-before-book, reviews that build a track record.

## 3. Where the field is weak (TradeVerified's openings)

1. **No trusted national brand.** Everyone is early or regional. The category is wide open.
2. **Verification is shallow or opaque.** Most show a "verified" badge but don't show *how* it was verified. Trust is asserted, not proven.
3. **Reviews are weak.** Generic star ratings that anyone can game; few require proof of service.
4. **Fragmented value.** Some are job boards, some are social networks, some are homeowner marketplaces — **no one combines network + verified reviews + job board + employer tools** as cleanly as LinkedIn does for office work.
5. **Clunky, corporate UX.** Many feel like they weren't designed phone-first for someone on a job site.

## 4. TradeVerified's positioning — how to win

> **"The verified professional network for the trades — where your license, your work, and your reputation are all provably real."**

Win on **four pillars**:

### Pillar 1 — Provable trust (the wedge)
Don't just show a badge. Show **how** every credential was verified: source (state board / issuer), reference number, date, and who verified it. This is the #1 differentiator and it's the feature being built now (see `verification` system below). Competitors say "verified" — TradeVerified *shows the receipt*.

### Pillar 2 — Worker-first pricing
Free to build a profile, get verified, and apply to unlimited jobs. Pro ($19/mo) is optional visibility only. This maximizes supply, which is what makes the platform valuable to employers.

### Pillar 3 — Proof-backed reviews
Reviews only from verified customers with proof of service. A rating on TradeVerified means more than five stars anywhere else.

### Pillar 4 — Local density, then expand
Win **one trade in one metro** (e.g., HVAC in one city) to reach liquidity, then expand trade-by-trade and city-by-city. A dense local network beats a thin national one.

### Go-to-market wedge
Partner with **trade schools and certification bodies** (like BlueRecruit) to seed verified, job-ready workers — they arrive pre-credentialed, which feeds Pillar 1 instantly.

> The category exists, but it's fragmented and trust is only skin-deep. **TradeVerified wins by making trust *provable*, keeping work free for tradespeople, and combining the network + reviews + job board + employer tools that competitors only do in pieces — starting dense in one trade and one city.**
