# Competitive notes

TradeVerified sits between a general job board and a home-services marketplace. The gap those products leave is a trades profile where a license is a checked document, not a line of text.

| Product | What it is good at | What it does not do for this buyer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| LinkedIn | Profiles, applications, employer search | Licenses, certifications, and job-site portfolios are an afterthought |
| Indeed and ZipRecruiter | High volume of job posts | Resumes are unstructured. A fake license number looks like a real one |
| Thumbtack and Angi | Homeowners hiring a pro for one job | Built for leads and ads, not for a contractor hiring a W-2 tech or a crew |
| Facebook groups and Craigslist | Local speed | No credential record, no application trail |
| State license lookups | The official source | Employers have to leave the hiring tool and check one person at a time |

## Why a contractor would try this

They already lose time on applicants who claim a journeyman card or an EPA 608 and cannot produce it. A profile that shows the document, the issuer, and whether a reviewer accepted it is the part LinkedIn does not have.

## Why a worker would try this

They are tired of being compared only on price with people whose credentials were never checked. The free apply path matters. Charging workers to submit an application would send them back to Indeed.

## What not to copy

- Lead-selling marketplaces, where the worker pays per introduction
- Star ratings with no tie to a finished job
- A verified badge that is purchased rather than reviewed

The pilot proves the directory, the job, the application, and the manual review. Automated board lookups and paid plans come after a few real hires, as described in `ROADMAP.md` and `VERIFICATION.md`.
